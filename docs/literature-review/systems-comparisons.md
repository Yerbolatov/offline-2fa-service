# Comparison of Authentication and Identity Systems

## Methodology

This comparison examines five systems that can be used as a central component for MFA or access to internal applications: privacyIDEA, Keycloak, FreeIPA, Duo, and Authelia. “Yes” means the feature is documented; “partially” means the feature can be obtained via an adapter, a separate component, or a limited scenario; and “no” means the feature is not available in the system’s standard configuration. The links in the cells lead to the manufacturer's or project's documentation. For privacyIDEA, Keycloak, FreeIPA, and Authelia, the self-hosted option is considered; Duo is a cloud-based service.

## Analysis of the Results

privacyIDEA is the closest fit for managing OTP tokens: it is standalone and supports TOTP, WebAuthn, LDAP, and auditing. Its limitation for this project is that it is a more comprehensive token management product, and integration with a reverse proxy or OIDC must be configured separately.

Keycloak is at its strongest as an OIDC/SSO platform and supports TOTP, WebAuthn, LDAP, and audit events. However, its realm/client/federation model and production infrastructure make it a cumbersome solution for a small, standalone service if only local login, TOTP, and forward authentication are required.

FreeIPA is well-suited for organizations that need a full-featured Linux identity management (IdM) solution with Kerberos, LDAP, and a certificate authority (CA). It is not a ready-made web-forward-auth/OIDC gateway and does not provide a built-in WebAuthn workflow, so it does not align well with the project’s MVP.

Duo offers robust reporting and a wide range of features, but its reliance on the cloud violates a key requirement for an isolated network. Offline passcodes on the client do not make Duo a fully autonomous server: management and verification remain tied to the Duo platform.

Authelia most closely resembles a reverse-proxy scenario: it supports forward authentication, OIDC, TOTP, WebAuthn, LDAP, and on-premises deployment. However, it is designed for an existing reverse proxy and policy gateway, rather than for the specialized management of TOTP secrets, backup codes, administrative resets, and immutable auditing required by the project proposal.

## Design conclusion

The comparison does not suggest that existing products are functionally “weak.” On the contrary, each of them covers a broader range of use cases: IdM, SSO, tokens, cloud MFA, or reverse-proxy gateways. The need for a custom solution is justified by the narrow overlap of the project’s requirements:

1. Fully local operation without an external cloud or a mandatory LDAP/Kerberos context;
2. a simple deployment in an isolated network with internal CAs and NTP;
3. TOTP-first MVP with reuse control, rate limiting, backup codes, and administrative reset;
4. built-in REST and forward-auth interfaces for internal applications;
5. A minimal footprint and straightforward audit instead of a full-fledged IdM platform.

Our solution is not intended to compete with Keycloak or FreeIPA as a general-purpose identity provider. Its purpose is to provide a verifiable, standalone MFA workflow that can be deployed with a single command and, if necessary, extended to include WebAuthn/OIDC once the basic TOTP scenario has been stabilized.
